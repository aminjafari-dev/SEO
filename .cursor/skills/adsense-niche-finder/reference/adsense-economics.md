# AdSense Economics for Niche Sites

## Revenue formula

```
Monthly revenue ≈ (Monthly pageviews ÷ 1000) × Page RPM
Page RPM ≈ Ad impressions per pageview × Fill rate × CPM ÷ 1000 × 1000
```

Practical version: estimate pageviews from the demand analysis, pick an RPM tier from below, multiply.

Projection table to include in every deep dive: revenue at 10,000 / 30,000 / 100,000 pageviews per month using the niche's low and high RPM.

RPM is driven by three things:
1. **Advertiser competition for the audience** (CPC of related commercial keywords). Expensive products and services → high CPC → high RPM.
2. **Geography of visitors.** US, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany pay several times more than most of Asia, Africa, and South America. A niche whose searchers are mostly in low-CPC countries will have a low RPM even if the topic is "expensive."
3. **Page layout and engagement.** Long-form articles with several in-content ad slots, high time-on-page, and low bounce earn more per view. Informational how-to content typically supports 3–6 ad units per page.

## Approximate AdSense Page RPM tiers (US traffic, informational content)

Treat these as orientation, not facts. Verify with recent niche-site income reports and ad-network published data when possible.

- **Tier A, $25–$60+**: insurance, loans, credit, legal, B2B software, web hosting, real estate, solar, HVAC, home security. Mostly YMYL or brand-dominated; usually fails the beatability gate for a new site. Sub-niches at the edge (e.g., very specific software how-tos, niche home services) can pass.
- **Tier B, $12–$25**: home improvement and repair, tools and equipment, automotive repair, personal finance-adjacent lifestyle (budgeting tools, not advice), pets (especially vet-adjacent care, breeds, gear), parenting products, travel planning, education/careers, tech tutorials, gardening equipment, outdoor gear, weddings.
- **Tier C, $6–$12**: general hobbies and crafts, cooking and recipes, fitness equipment, fashion, beauty, home decor, sports equipment, music gear, photography, board games, aquarium and reptile keeping, sewing/knitting, woodworking.
- **Tier D, $1–$6**: entertainment, celebrity, quotes, memes, gaming news, anime, lyrics, free downloads, generic "facts" content, and any niche dominated by teenage or low-income-country traffic.

Sweet spot for this project: Tier B and upper Tier C niches that are informational, non-YMYL, and have low competition. A Tier C niche with 3x the beatable demand usually beats a Tier B niche you cannot rank in.

## Proxies for RPM when you have no data

- **Ad count** on commercial-adjacent queries in the cluster ("best X", "X price", "X for sale", "X service near me"). 3–4 ads → strong advertiser demand. 0 ads across the whole cluster → expect Tier D.
- **Product price level**: if the niche revolves around $200–$2,000 products or paid services, advertisers pay more.
- **Google Keyword Planner top-of-page bid** (free with a Google Ads account, no spend needed) for 5 commercial keywords in the niche. Average bid over $1.50 suggests Tier B or better.
- **Ad network niche reports**: Mediavine, Raptive, Ezoic publish periodic RPM-by-category data. Search for the most recent.
- **Sold-site listings**: Flippa, Empire Flippers, Motion Invest list monthly pageviews and revenue; divide to get real RPMs for comparable niches.

## Upgrade path beyond AdSense

Premium networks pay 2–4x AdSense RPM. Their entry thresholds change; verify current requirements. Historically:
- Mediavine: ~50,000 sessions/month (a lower-tier program has existed around 10,000 sessions).
- Raptive (formerly AdThrive): ~100,000 pageviews/month, mostly US traffic.
- Ezoic: no hard minimum, moderate uplift.
Factor this into the "ceiling" of a niche: a Tier C niche with big US traffic becomes attractive once it qualifies for a premium network.

## AdSense policy gates (hard FAIL)

Prohibited: sexually explicit content, child endangerment, dangerous or derogatory content, enabling dishonest behavior (hacking, cheating, fake documents), counterfeit goods, illegal drugs and paraphernalia, weapons and explosives instructions, content that misrepresents itself, copyrighted material you do not own (lyrics, movies, software downloads).

Restricted (allowed only with approval or with limited advertiser demand): alcohol, gambling and betting, tobacco and vaping, recreational drugs including cannabis, healthcare and medicines, sexual and adult themes, shocking content, political content in some markets. Treat restricted categories as FAIL for a first site.

Additional approval realities: a site typically needs an About page, Contact page, Privacy Policy, original content across 20–30+ articles, clear navigation, and no under-construction pages before AdSense approval. Sites in high-abuse niches (downloads, streaming, coupons) get rejected more often.

## Google Search quality gates

- YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) topics — health, medicine, finance, law, safety, civic information, and content affecting major life decisions — are held to E-E-A-T standards that a new anonymous site cannot meet. Treat as FAIL.
- Helpful content and scaled content policies: sites that look like AI-spun content farms get suppressed. The niche must allow first-hand experience, original images, or original data; otherwise the site is fragile.
- Site reputation abuse and expired-domain abuse policies make shortcuts (buying old domains, parasite hosting) risky; the plan assumes a fresh domain and honest content.

## AI Overviews and zero-click: why they matter for ad revenue

Display-ad revenue depends on the visit. A query fully answered by an AI Overview, a featured snippet, or an instant answer pays nothing, regardless of ranking.

Estimate AIO exposure for a niche as the share of its keyword cluster that shows an AI Overview. Typical impact on organic CTR where AIO is present: roughly 30–60% reduction, larger for short factual questions, smaller for complex or experience-based queries.

Query types most resistant to AI answers (prefer niches rich in these):
- Personal experience and opinion ("is it worth it", "what I wish I knew").
- Long comparisons with many variables that need a table or images.
- Visual identification (breeds, plants, parts, symbols, patterns).
- Step-by-step processes where the reader follows along on the page.
- Tools, calculators, converters, checklists, templates, printables.
- Frequently changing data (prices, availability, versions, schedules).
- Local and regional specifics.
- Troubleshooting with many branches ("X not working", error codes).

Query types most exposed (avoid niches dominated by these): definitions, single facts, dates, conversions, short "what is" and "how many" questions, quick yes/no answers.
